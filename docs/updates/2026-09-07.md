# 2026-09-07

## Showreel moved into the hero

The selected-work showreel now sits in the middle of the hero, centred and
stacked above the three statement lines, instead of occupying its own orange
section below the fold.

### What changed

**`app/page.tsx`** — the showreel's `<section id="showreel">` is gone. The
component now renders inside `<section id="hero">`, which became a centring
flex container. The `id="showreel"` moved onto the inner wrapper so an
existing `#showreel` link still resolves.

The hero gained `pt-20 lg:pt-section-tablet`, putting back exactly the space
the negative margin takes away, so the rectangle centres in the area below
the fixed header rather than in the full viewport box. `min-h-svh` is a
minimum rather than a fixed height: on a short viewport the video plus its
caption is taller than the space available, and the section grows instead of
overflowing into the one below.

`z-10` on the wrapper is what puts the rectangle over the statement lines.
The mouse-parallax lines pass behind its edges, which is intended.

**`app/_components/showreel-pixels.tsx`** — the cover cells changed from
`bg-primary-300` to `bg-surface-inverse`, matching the shape wrapper the
video sits in.

The old fill existed to match the enclosing section exactly, so the cover was
invisible until the cells cleared. That is not achievable in the hero, for
two independent reasons:

- The rectangle spans a *band* of the hero's cream-to-orange gradient —
  roughly its middle 60% on a typical desktop — not a single point, so no
  flat colour can match it.
- The fill has to stay flat regardless. Every cell starts as a circle at
  `PIXEL_CELL_SCALE` (1.42), overlapping its neighbours, and only reads as
  solid because all 144 are one colour. Giving each cell a slice of the
  gradient would show 144 mismatched, magnified tiles.

Matching the gradient would mean rebuilding the cover as a single
gradient-filled element behind an SVG mask of 144 circles. That was judged
disproportionate. A dark rectangle dissolving into video reads as
intentional; a near-miss orange would read as a bug.

**`app/_components/showreel.tsx`** — the cover watchdog is now gated on
`introSettled` as well as `visible`.

It previously started its `PIXEL_COVER_MAX_MS` timer the moment the section
became visible. That was safe while the showreel was below the fold, because
visibility could not happen until after the hero entrance had settled. Inside
the hero, `visible` is true at first paint while `introSettled` is still
3850ms away, so the timer would have spent most of its budget before the
entrance was even allowed to start, then fired partway through the morph —
tearing the cover off mid-animation, which is the one thing it exists to
prevent.

Timing from the later of the two restores the intended budget: the whole
entrance plus three seconds of slack, measured from the first moment the
reveal could actually run.

### Sequencing

No new sequencing was written. The showreel's entrance already waited on
`subscribeIntroSettled` in `app/_lib/intro.ts`, which `finish()` in
`hero-intro.tsx` raises when the hero timeline completes — and that timeline
is held open past the navbar by a trailing empty tween, so it completes at
`INTRO.total` (3850ms) = `navbarStart` (1850) + `navbarDuration` (2000).
The entrance therefore begins after the navbar has finished arriving.

## Showreel cover reworked after visual review

The dark cover above did not survive looking at it: `bg-surface-inverse`
(`#11100e`) at that size on a cream-to-orange hero read as a hole punched in
the page, and because the entrance waits on the hero settling at 3850ms it sat
there for roughly four seconds before anything moved. Two changes, same day.

**The cover is now `bg-primary-200`** — a soft peach inside the band of the
gradient the rectangle covers. Not invisible, and not trying to be; the two
reasons invisibility is unreachable are unchanged and recorded in the spec.

**The rectangle paints nothing until the entrance is armed.** `aspect-video`
still reserves the space and the hero gradient shows straight through it. The
cover and the video's frame appear in the same commit that begins the reveal,
so the cover is only ever on screen while dissolving. `coverArmed` latches
during render rather than in an effect — an effect would commit one paint
later and the video would flash uncovered, and `react-hooks/set-state-in-effect`
rejects it besides.

The cover watchdog moved from being gated on the cover to being gated on
`mayLoad`, so it still fires when the entrance never arms at all. Without that
change a refused autoplay would have left a permanent transparent hole in the
hero instead of a permanent block.

Circular pixel mechanics are untouched.

### One regression caught during verification

The first version of the visibility condition was `coverArmed || revealDone`.
That renders the shape wrapper with `opacity-0` on the server, and **nothing
flips it without JavaScript** — so a no-JS visitor got an invisible rectangle
instead of the poster. Reduced motion failed the same way: `mayLoad` is false,
so the watchdog never runs and `revealDone` never arrives.

The condition is now `!mayLoad || coverArmed || revealDone`. This was not
caught by lint, by `tsc`, or by looking at the page in a normal browser — only
by reading the generated HTML, where the server-rendered wrapper must carry
`opacity-100`.

## Entrance inverted: mask-in instead of cover-off

Confirmed working in Chrome and Safari via the probe route below, so the
rewrite went ahead.

**The video's container is now masked rather than covered.** 144 `<rect>` cells
in an SVG `<mask>` start at opacity 0 — so the rectangle is genuinely
transparent and the hero gradient shows through it — then fade in from the
centre outward, each resolving from a circle into a square as it arrives. The
film materialises out of the page instead of an overlay dissolving off it.

This removes the cover-colour problem outright. A cover must be painted some
colour and the background is a gradient, so there was never a right answer;
both attempts proved it, one reading as a bug and the other as a hole. A mask
has no fill.

**The circle-to-rectangle morph is gone**, at the user's request. With it went
the clip-path measurement, the `min()` diameter derivation, the runtime
`borderTopLeftRadius` read, and the Chrome two-value-inset workaround — a
paragraph of hard-won detail that no longer has anything to describe. The
entrance is now 1100ms of pixels and nothing else.

The per-cell circle-to-square is kept.

### What got simpler

- `maskContentUnits="objectBoundingBox"` means coordinates are fractions of the
  masked element, so there is **no measurement and no resize handling** at all.
  The clip-path morph needed both.
- `settleShape()` is deleted. Dropping the mask is what returns the video to
  normal, and `revealDone` already unmounts it.
- Cells are grown via their geometry attributes rather than transformed. A
  `scale()` inside an objectBoundingBox mask would have to reason about a
  non-uniform user space and a transform origin; geometry has neither problem.
- The shape wrapper lost `bg-surface-inverse`. Nothing sits behind the video
  that needs hiding.
- Rounded corners survive for free: `rounded-lg` and `overflow-hidden` clip the
  video before the mask applies, so nothing snaps when the mask is dropped.

### One new failure route

Six now end the mask rather than five. The entrance effect being cleaned up
mid-flight — a scroll away, or an unmount — cannot set `revealDone`, so instead
of removing the mask it **completes** it: every cell forced to its finished
square at full opacity, which makes the mask solid white and therefore a no-op.
A mask only has to be finished to stop hiding things, where a cover had to be
removed from the DOM.

### Not verified

The animation itself has not been watched — only the constants and the built
output were checked.

### Follow-up: cell scale lowered to 1.05

After seeing it run, `PIXEL_CELL_SCALE` went from the inherited 1.42 to 1.05.

The 1.42 was a coverage requirement for the cover it replaced — a circle only
hides a square if its diameter is that square's diagonal — and revealing has no
such constraint. At 1.42 the round cells overlapped heavily and the image
filled in before the circles could be read as circles at all. Just over 1 keeps
them distinct and lets them close the gaps only as they square up.

Not exactly 1, so neighbours still meet as they resolve rather than touching
only at the last moment of each cell's tween. Only the start state is affected:
every cell finishes at its own slot exactly whatever this is set to, so the
finished mask always tiles perfectly.

## Hero back to 100svh: showreel sized grid-first, height-capped

Widening the rectangle to `max-w-page` had pushed the hero past its `min-h-svh`
floor by 100-400px on every desktop size, so the section below stopped
beginning at the fold. A `pb-40 lg:pb-60` tried as a way to lift the showreel
made it worse: there was no slack to centre in, so it moved nothing and added
240px of height at `lg`. Reverted.

The rectangle is now sized

```
width: min(100%, (100svh - var(--showreel-reserve)) * 16 / 9)
```

on the block holding both the video and its caption, so the two always share a
width. The grid width is an upper bound; the height cap takes over when there
is not room for it. `--showreel-reserve` is 15rem, 17rem from `lg`.

Width is constrained rather than height on purpose: with a definite width,
`aspect-ratio` derives the height, so a `max-height` would clamp the box
shorter than its own ratio and `object-cover` would crop the film.

**The two goals genuinely collide at 16:9.** A gutter-aligned rectangle on a
1440x900 screen is 1280x720, needing about 950px of a 900px viewport once the
caption row and header clearance are counted. Computed across ordinary desktop
sizes the rectangle lands at 82-93% of the grid width and clears the fold by
about 36px; full alignment returns only on viewports tall enough to afford it,
such as 2560x1440.

### This also mostly closes the upscale question

The displayed width now lands between 880 and 1440 CSS px across ordinary
desktop sizes — at or under the 1280px source almost everywhere, about 1.12x at
1920x1080, against the 1.4x it reached at full grid width. Only a very large
and very tall screen still pushes it hard: 2560x1440 hits the `max-w-page` cap
at 1920 CSS px, a 1.5x upscale. Re-encoding at 1080p remains the answer if that
becomes a target.

## Circle-to-square made visible

The shape change was in the code but never on the screen. A cell faded in and
squared up in the same 160ms tween, so it was never a visible circle at any
point and the effect read as squares simply appearing.

Split into three phases, each its own constant:

| | Constant | Was |
|---|---|---|
| appear, still round | `PIXEL_FADE_MS` 200ms | part of one 160ms tween |
| hold as a dot | `PIXEL_DOT_HOLD_MS` 200ms | did not exist |
| circle to square | `PIXEL_MORPH_MS` 550ms, `power2.inOut` | part of the same 160ms tween, linear |

The hold is the fix. Without it the morph begins the instant the cell is
visible and the eye still reads one continuous event rather than a circle that
then becomes a square.

Both tweens share one stagger function, so a cell's morph is timed from its own
arrival rather than from the grid finishing — the wave of dots and the wave of
squares chase each other across the rectangle.

`PIXEL_REVEAL_MS` went 1100 to 1300 and now covers only the fade-in scatter;
`ENTRANCE_MS` is derived rather than equal to it, at 2050ms. That feeds
`PIXEL_COVER_MAX_MS` and the reveal watchdog, both of which scale with it.

Since the entrance starts when the hero settles at 3850ms, the video is now
fully resolved at roughly 5.9s from load.

## Showreel entrance cued two seconds earlier

The showreel waited for the hero timeline to complete, which meant waiting out
the navbar's full 2000ms transition — putting the video's entrance at roughly
5.9s from load. Nothing about the showreel depends on the navbar having
arrived.

The timeline now raises the cue at `INTRO.showreelCue` (1850ms, the same moment
as `navbarStart`) and carries on. The navbar's 2000ms and the showreel's 2050ms
run together, finishing within 50ms of each other at about **3.9s**.

**The navbar is unchanged.** `navbarStart`, `navbarDuration` and `total` are
all the same values; the cue is a `.call` that fires and returns, sitting
immediately before the navbar's own `.call` at the identical position.
Confirmed in the built bundle:
`navbarStart:1850,navbarDuration:2e3,showreelCue:1850,total:3850`.

### The signal was renamed

`markIntroSettled` / `subscribeIntroSettled` became `markShowreelCue` /
`subscribeShowreelCue`, and the showreel's `introSettled` state became
`heroCued`. The old name described the moment the intro finished, which is no
longer when this fires — leaving it would have been a lie in the code.

`isIntroSettled()` was exported and unused; deleted.

`finish()` still raises the cue as a backstop for every path where the timeline
never reaches 1850ms — reduced motion, a failed import, the watchdog, a load
the intro does not play on. Idempotent, so whichever comes first wins.

## Caption fades and rises in after the entrance

The statement and the control group now lift into place — `translate-y-6
opacity-0` to `translate-y-0 opacity-100`, 700ms on `--ease-out`, the controls
200ms behind the statement.

Driven by a new `captionVisible`, which is the rectangle's own rule one term
shorter:

```
rectangleVisible = !mayLoad || coverArmed || revealDone
captionVisible   = !mayLoad              || revealDone
```

Dropping `coverArmed` is the whole point: the text stays down for the entire
pixel entrance and arrives after it, rather than competing with it.

**Behaviour change worth noting:** the caption is now hidden during the hero
intro too. Before this it sat there in full while the rectangle was still
transparent.

CSS transitions, not GSAP - two elements reacting to one flag, no sequencing to
coordinate, no JavaScript. `opacity` and `translate` are named explicitly
rather than `transition-all`, which would also catch the controls' colour
transitions and fight them. The mobile navigation already uses this pattern.

Verified in the generated CSS that `translate-y-*` compiles to the `translate`
property in Tailwind v4, so the named transition actually matches it, and that
`@property --tw-translate-x` carries an initial value of 0 - without it the
shorthand would be invalid and nothing would move. The server-rendered markup
carries `translate-y-0 opacity-100`, so no-JS and reduced motion still show the
text in place.

## Entrance replays on switch, and projects auto-advance

**Every project now arrives through the pixel entrance.** It was restricted to
LAGA, and a switch cancelled it. Pressing a control arms the mask in the same
commit as the switch, so the rectangle empties at once and the new film
materialises when it reports ready and playing.

`revealDone` became per project rather than per page load, `canReveal` lost its
`index === 0` term, and the mask is keyed on the project index so it remounts
with every cell back at opacity 0 — without the key a switch made mid-entrance
would show the new film in full before the pixels took it back.

The caption is deliberately excluded. It is latched on a new `captionShown`
that never clears, so it rises once and stays: the statement is fixed text that
says nothing about the current project, and dropping and re-raising it on every
click would be noise.

**Auto-advance at `AUTO_ADVANCE_MS` (12s)**, timed from the entrance settling
rather than the switch, so every project gets the same dwell however long its
media took to arrive. A full cycle is about 14s with the entrance. It stops
when off screen, under reduced motion, and on a failed film; a manual press
resets the clock.

## Switches hold the outgoing frame, and advance at 9s

`AUTO_ADVANCE_MS` is 9000 rather than 12000, so a project advances at about the
point its content ends instead of looping and cutting 3s into a replay. Close
rather than exact — the clips do not share one content length, and playback
starts before the dwell does.

**A switch no longer empties the rectangle.** The mask hides whatever it is
applied to, and it was applied to the wrapper holding everything, so the
outgoing film went with the incoming one and the rectangle blanked mid-switch.

The mask moved **inside** the shape wrapper, onto a layer carrying only the
video and its poster, and a canvas now sits unmasked beneath it:

```
shape wrapper      rounded-lg + overflow-hidden, opacity gate. No background.
  hold canvas      the outgoing frame. UNMASKED, hidden when idle
  masked layer     carries mask-image
    video
    poster
```

On a switch the frame on screen is drawn to that canvas synchronously inside
the click — before `src` changes, since assigning a new source blanks the
element immediately and the frame is gone. It is released on `revealDone`, in
the same commit the mask goes, so nothing is painted in between.

A canvas rather than a second `<video>`: two elements would mean two sets of
playback state and handlers, and an outgoing clip restarting from zero unless
its `currentTime` were carried across — a lot of machinery for two seconds of
picture.

**It is a held frame, not a crossfade.** The outgoing film stops rather than
playing on. A true crossfade remains unbuilt and `crossfadeAt` still unread.

`shapeRef` was deleted; nothing had read it since the clip-path morph went.

### Not verified

The transition has not been watched. Two things to judge by eye: whether a

## Advance driven by crossfadeAt, timer demoted to a watchdog

The carousel advanced on a fixed interval, which could not be made to fit.
Playback begins before the entrance finishes, so a clip is already about 2s in
by the time it is fully revealed, and that time counts against its content — at
a 7s dwell every clip looped back to `startAt` before switching, LAGA by nearly
two seconds against only 7.3s of content. Shortening the interval enough to
clear the shortest clip would have cut the others short.

`onTimeUpdate` now compares the element's real `currentTime` against
`crossfadeAt` and advances there. Exact per clip, and self-correcting when a
load runs slow:

| Project | Content | Advances at | Dwell after entrance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laga | 7.3s | 7.3s | 5.3s |
| Nourigo | 8.8s | 8.8s | 6.8s |
| INN News | 8.9s | 8.9s | 6.9s |
| Bitazza | 8.6s | 8.6s | 6.5s |

This is what `crossfadeAt` was measured for. It is the same value the custom
loop already compared against; reaching it now advances instead of looping,
whenever rotation is allowed. When it is not — entrance still running, off
screen, reduced motion, failed film — it loops as before, so the clip never
runs on into the blank end card.

`AUTO_ADVANCE_MS` became `ADVANCE_FALLBACK_MS` at 15s, timed from the entrance
settling, purely for the case where `crossfadeAt` never arrives: a stalled
download, a decode that stops reporting, a `timeupdate` that dries up. The
narrowest margin against a real advance is about 8s, and a real advance
rebuilds the timer before it can fire.

`onEnded` routes through the same decision. It should never fire, since every
`crossfadeAt` sits well before the media ends.

`captureHold` and `selectProject` moved above the media handlers so the
handlers can call them — the same use-before-declare rule that caught the
timer effect earlier.

## Mobile: full-height rectangle, single-line chip strip

**The rectangle fills the screen height below `md`** — `100svh -
var(--showreel-reserve)` at full width, rather than 16:9. `object-cover` crops
the film to the portrait box, so the frame changes shape and the film does not.
The width cap applies from `md` up only.

**The mask grid had to follow, and this was the non-obvious part.** Cell
geometry is in objectBoundingBox units, so a fixed 16 x 9 grid makes square
cells only in a 16:9 box — in a portrait box every cell becomes a tall
rectangle and every circle an ellipse.

`pixelGrid(aspect)` now picks the arrangement, holding the cell count near
`PIXEL_TARGET_CELLS` and varying the layout: `columns = round(sqrt(cells *
aspect))`, clamped 4-32. Cell aspect across real devices comes out 0.91-1.01,
against 0.32 for a fixed 16 x 9 grid on an iPhone 14.

A `ResizeObserver` on the position wrapper drives it rather than a media query
— the box's width comes from the page grid and its height from the viewport, so
the ratio moves continuously. The grid is replaced only on a real change, so a
drag does not remount the mask every frame.

`PIXEL_ORDER` became `pixelOrder(columns, rows)`, cached per arrangement, still
deterministic.

**The chips are one scrolling line below `md`**, with the active pill kept
centred so its neighbours stay visible and clickable. Four pills come to about
370px against roughly 340px of phone width, so they had been wrapping to two
rows — costing the rectangle vertical space.

Centring uses `scrollTo` on the strip rather than `scrollIntoView` on the pill:
the latter walks up the ancestor chain and can scroll the page, which on a
phone would jump the hero every time the showreel advanced on its own. Reduced
motion jumps instead of smooth-scrolling. Scrollbar hidden on both engines.

### Not verified

Nothing was loaded in a browser. The cell-aspect table is computed from the
tokens; the 370px pill estimate is an estimate, and if the strip turns out to
fit without scrolling then the centring is simply a no-op.

## Sticky gradient stage, flying statement lines, curtain transition

The hero's gradient and statement lines moved out of the hero section and into
a **sticky stage** that spans the hero and the section after it. The gradient
stays pinned to the top of the viewport while everything else scrolls over it,
the statement lines fly upward and out of frame as the page scrolls, and a
solid panel rolls up over the pinned background at the end.

### The stage arithmetic

A sticky element stays pinned for exactly `container height - its own height`
of scrolling. Pinning across two viewports therefore needs a container of
three, which is where the two odd-looking utilities come from:

```
<div class="relative -mt-20 lg:-mt-section-tablet">   stage, 200svh
  <div class="sticky top-0 h-svh">                    gradient — takes its own 100svh
  <div class="-mt-[100svh]">                          content, pulled back over it
    <section id="hero" class="min-h-svh">             showreel
    <section class="min-h-svh">                       test (placeholder)
  </div>
</div>
<section class="bg-linear-to-b from-primary-300 …">   next (placeholder)
```

The gradient takes its own 100svh of flow and the content is pulled back over
it, so the stage measures two viewports and the gradient is pinned for one —
the length of the hero. It then releases and travels up with the section below.

**The negative margin is on the content, never on the sticky element.** That
was a real bug for one revision: `margin-bottom: -100svh` on the gradient
zeroed its own flow height, which looked equivalent, but sticky constrains an
element's MARGIN box to its containing block rather than its border box. With a
zero-height margin box the visible box was free to overhang the stage by a full
viewport, so the gradient carried on travelling behind the section below and
the join landed on a mid-gradient peach instead of the end stop — a hard
horizontal seam, exactly what the seamless join was supposed to avoid.

**The seam is exact, not approximate.** The section after the stage starts on
`primary-300`, the gradient's own bottom stop, so where they meet there is no
line to see.

### The lines gained a transform layer

`hero-intro.tsx` went from four elements per line to five. The scroll flight
writes to the new layer 2 and nothing else — putting it on an existing layer
would fold it into that layer's matrix and lose the tilt, which is what the
layer split has always existed to prevent:

```
1 position   absolute placement. Nothing animates it.
2 flight     scroll-driven y.                      NEW
3 entrance   the intro timeline's y.
4 parallax   mouse-driven x and y.
5 type       typography and the tilt. Never a GSAP target.
```

Gated on `entranceDone`, so scrolling during the first four seconds cannot have
the lines falling in and flying out at once, and inside a
`prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` matchMedia block like the parallax.

A plain scroll listener with a `quickSetter`, not ScrollTrigger: the value is a
direct function of `scrollY` with no easing, timeline or pinning involved, and
the plugin would be 40kB to compute one number. Writes are coalesced to one per
frame, and it runs once on mount so a reload partway down the page does not
start the lines at zero and jump.

### A silent failure caught by the build

`-mb-svh` and `-mt-svh` were the first attempt. They compiled to **nothing** —
`svh` is a height keyword in Tailwind v4, not a spacing value, so the margin
utilities matched no theme entry and produced no CSS and no error. The whole
stage would have collapsed with no diagnostic. `-mb-[100svh]` and
`-mt-[100svh]` are the working form, both confirmed in the generated
stylesheet.

### Four corrections after seeing it run

**White band above the gradient on load.** The negative top margin that pulls
the hero under the header stayed on the hero when the gradient moved out to the
stage — so the stage began *below* the header and the page's own background
showed above the gradient. The offset moved to the stage, which is what the
gradient's top edge actually follows; the hero keeps its compensating `pt-20`.

**The lines left too early.** The flight was spread over one viewport, so it
was finished by the time the second section arrived. It now runs over
`SCROLL_FLIGHT_VIEWPORTS` (2) — the whole length of the stage.

The distances came down twice, 1.5/1.8/2.1 to 1.2/1.45/1.7 to **0.7/0.85/1.0**,
and are now all under 1 on purpose. The flight is movement RELATIVE TO the
layer the lines sit in, and that layer is only pinned for the hero — after that
it travels up with the page, so a line's total distance is its own flight plus
the layer's. Distances that cleared the frame unaided took the lines out before
the section below arrived.

**No curtain, and the pin is one viewport, not two.** The first attempt held
the gradient still while the next section rose over it. That was wrong: it read
as two things moving against each other rather than one picture leaving.

The gradient is now pinned for the length of the hero only. When the section
below reaches the top of the viewport the pin ends, and from there the gradient
travels up with it — they leave together as one picture. Everything that
existed to support the roll is gone with it: the 100svh tail inside the stage,
and the `-mt-[100svh]` that pulled the following section back over it. The
stage is two viewports showing two, and the section after it is ordinary flow.

The section below is not pinned either. It scrolls, as it should.

**The handover is two sections, not one.** The first holds solid
`primary-300`, the hero gradient's exact bottom stop, so the join with the
stage is invisible. The second runs `from-primary-300 to-surface-base`, so the
page arrives back at its own background rather than stopping dead on brand
orange.

Splitting them keeps the first join reading as one continuous surface: a
section that began fading immediately would put a gradient straight against the
stage's own gradient, and the change of rate would show even where the colours
matched.

**Three joins now depend on `primary-300` being identical in all of them** —
the hero gradient's end stop, the solid section, and the `from-` of the fading
one. Change one and all three have to move together.

## The pixels were ellipses, not circles

`pixelGrid` rounds columns and rows to integers, so cells are only
approximately square — up to about 11% off. `rx` and `ry` were taken as half of
each cell's own side, which fed that error straight into the shape. It did not
show while cells were ~40px and the box was a fixed 16:9; once the ratio was
dropped the cells grew to 75-100px and the error became several visible pixels
of difference between the axes.

| Viewport | Cell | rx / ry before | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1024x768 | 56x60 | 28 x 30 (7% off) | 28 x 28 |
| 1512x982 | 80x87 | 40 x 43 (8% off) | 40 x 40 |
| 1440x900 | 75x76 | 38 x 38 (2% off) | 38 x 38 |

The start shape is now sized in PIXELS and converted back: the diameter comes
from the shorter side of the cell, both axes get that same length, and the rect
is square on screen whatever shape its cell is. `finished` still fills the cell
exactly, so the morph ends on a grid that tiles — only the start is a circle.

### Verified along the way

Function-based values inside GSAP's `attr` were an assumption I had never
checked. They are supported: `_addPropTween` in the installed `gsap-core.js`
opens with `_isFunction(end) && (end = end(index || 0, target, targets))`, and
AttrPlugin passes `index` and `targets` through. So the per-cell geometry was
being written correctly all along — the values themselves were the problem.

## The automatic advance no longer jumps either

The alignment added for presses lived in the click handler, so the automatic
advance never went through it — and the wrap from BITAZZA back to LAGA is the
longest journey on the strip, a whole copy-width leftwards.

`alignFromCopy` became `alignToNearestCopy` and moved into `selectProject`, so
every route a switch can take is covered: a press, `crossfadeAt` advancing, and
the stall fallback alike. Instead of comparing against the pressed element it
finds whichever copy of the incoming pill is nearest the middle of the strip —
which is the pressed one for a press, and the next copy along for a wrap.

| Switch | Nearest copy | Travel before | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| wrap, BITAZZA to LAGA | 2 | 289px back | 89px forward |
| LAGA to NOURIGO | 1 (no shift) | 87px | 87px |
| INN NEWS to BITAZZA | 1 (no shift) | 103px | 103px |

Ordinary advances need no shift — the middle copy is already nearest — so the
mechanism costs nothing except where it is needed. A shift that would push the
strip outside its scroll range is refused rather than clamped.

The click handler is back to a plain `selectProject` call, and one mechanism
now does what two did.

## Chip strip: full-bleed, and pressing a copy no longer jumps

**Pressing a pill in an outer copy sent the strip travelling backwards.** The
pill that becomes active is the middle copy's, a whole copy-width away, so the
centring smooth-scrolled all the way across — press LAGA on the right and the
row ran left past everything.

Copies are identical, so `alignFromCopy` now moves the strip by the distance
between the pressed pill and its real twin before the scroll begins. The real
pill lands under exactly the pixels the finger touched, so nothing appears to
move, and the smooth scroll that follows travels a short distance in whichever
direction is nearest. Worst case worked through — BITAZZA centred, LAGA pressed
on the right — travel goes from 289px backwards to 89px forwards, and the
aligned position stays inside the scroll range.

`scrollLeft` is written directly rather than through `scrollTo`: it has to land
in the same frame as the press, and any easing there would be the jump it
exists to hide.

**The strip is now the only thing that leaves the page grid.**
`-mx-gutter-mobile` pulls it out to the full screen width so the pills are
clipped by the screen edges instead of stopping short inside the margin. The
rectangle above keeps its gutter, unchanged. Cancelled at `md`.

## Chip strip loops; full-bleed rectangle reverted

The full-bleed experiment below was reverted the same day — the rectangle keeps
the page gutters and its rounded corners at every size, and the statement no
longer carries a gutter of its own. Only the pill strip changed.

**The strip now carries three copies of the set.** Centring the active pill in
a single copy left the first and last with a wide blank on one side; the end
padding tried first closed that only by replacing it with a different blank. A
copy either side means there is always a pill where the eye expects one.

A loop in appearance, not behaviour: nothing wraps around, the middle copy is
kept centred and the outer two show past its ends. Against a 342px strip one
copy is 370px, `scrollWidth` 1126, usable range 0-784, and the four targets
fall at 242, 329, 429 and 531 — all well inside, so nothing clamps.

**The copies cost nothing in the accessibility tree.** `aria-hidden` and out of
the tab order, so it still holds exactly four controls, each announcing its
project once, and `aria-current` is true of one element rather than three.
Verified in the generated HTML: 12 pills, 8 hidden, one `aria-current`.
`md:hidden` keeps them out of the wrapped desktop layout.

The end-padding logic is gone with it; the centring effect is just a measure
and a `scrollTo` again.

## Superseded: rectangle runs to the screen edge, every pill centres

**Full bleed below `md`.** The gutter moved off the container and onto the
statement, which still needs it; the pill strip supplies its own. From `md` up
it returns to the container and everything lines up with the header again.
Corners are square below `md` — a rounded corner against the screen edge shows
a wedge of hero gradient outside it and reads as a card that has overflowed.

**The first and last pills now reach the centre.** A scroller cannot centre its
end items on its own; there is nothing past them to scroll into. The strip is
padded at each end by half its own width minus half the end pill, which gives
the first exactly enough room to its left and the last exactly enough to its
right.

Checked against a 342px strip and the four real pills: lead 136px, trail 125px,
`scrollWidth` 631. The first centres at `scrollLeft` 0, the last at 289 —
exactly `scrollWidth - clientWidth`. Both ends land on the scroll limits, which
is what makes the padding exact rather than approximate.

Padding rather than duplicating the list, which was the other option
considered: a cloned set would put four more real buttons in the accessibility
tree announcing projects already present, and `aria-current` would be true of
two elements at once.

The figures are computed because they depend on rendered widths CSS cannot
name. A `ResizeObserver` recomputes them and clears them when the layout
crosses `md`; `getComputedStyle(...).overflowX` decides whether the strip is
scrolling, rather than a second copy of the breakpoint. Offsets are measured
from rendered rectangles rather than `offsetLeft`, which is relative to the
nearest positioned ancestor and not the strip.

## Temporary route: SVG mask support probe

`app/dev/mask-test/` was added to answer one question before committing to a
rewrite of the showreel entrance: whether `mask-image: url(#inline-svg-mask)`
resolves on an HTML element in the browsers this project targets.

**Answered: it works in both, and the route has been deleted.** The rewrite
above went ahead on that result; `app/dev/` no longer exists.

The proposal was to invert the entrance — instead of an opaque cover dissolving
off the video, mask the video's own container so the film materialises out of
the hero gradient, and drop the circle-to-rectangle morph entirely. That
removes the cover, the clip-path measurement and the Chrome two-value-inset
workaround, and needs no resize handling because the mask uses
`objectBoundingBox` units. It rests entirely on the support question.

`CSS.supports` cannot answer it — it parses the declaration and returns true
for a reference that never resolves. The page therefore shows five rendered
probes, each captioned with what working and not working look like: an
unprefixed mask on a plain element, the `-webkit-` variant, the real video with
both properties, the full animated entrance, and an SVG `foreignObject`
fallback.

It was never linked from anywhere, stayed out of the sitemap because it was
absent from `publicRoutes` (confirmed against the generated `sitemap.xml`), and
carried `noindex, nofollow`. Deleted the same day, once it had answered the
question. Nothing references it.
