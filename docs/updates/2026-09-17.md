# 17 September 2026

Insight cards brought up to the situation cards' behaviour, a wide-screen fault
in the pixel grid fixed at its root, and a two-ink wipe built for the CTA band.
Three of the items below record a wrong first attempt, because in each case the
wrong one is the more useful thing to have written down.

## The insight cards

They had no reveal, no hover, no link and no picture. They now have all four,
and where a behaviour already existed elsewhere on the site it was copied rather
than reinvented — the "Where are you now?" cards are the reference throughout.

### The picture

There are no insight photographs in the repository: the `Insight` type says only
Sanity supplies them, and the static articles are planned rather than written.
Rather than borrow client work and imply an association the articles do not
have, the stencil is now a **soft diagonal wash** — the ink colour at full
strength falling to 55% — instead of a flat blob. It invents no content and
needs no asset.

### The whole card turns over

A real 180-degree flip with two faces, not a CSS tilt. **It rests at 180deg**,
which is the part that is easy to get wrong and is argued at length in
`use-situation-cards.ts`: the face carrying `rotate-y-180` has come round a full
360 by the end, so it faces the reader squarely and is not mirrored.

The back is brand orange with the monogram centred, masked so it takes a flat
colour, at `mask-size: contain` — the stencil's `100% 100%` is exact only
because that file and its box share a ratio.

**A first attempt flipped only the picture**, which left the card's background
standing still. Both faces now share ONE grid cell rather than being absolutely
positioned: a content card has no fixed ratio, so the front defines the height
from its own words and the back stretches to match. Absolute faces collapse the
box to nothing.

**Neither face may carry `overflow-hidden`, and a mask counts as one.** A
non-visible overflow renders an element into its own flattened plane, which can
take it out of the parent's 3D context and stop `backface-visibility` applying —
and when that fails BOTH faces draw and the card rests looking mirrored. The
stencil's clip and mask moved onto an inner element inside the face.

### The arrival, and a miss worth recording

The cards were scouted first, their figures written up, and then **a fraction of
them implemented**: the flip was copied and the rest quietly dropped. Reported
as "why you copy it wrong?", and fairly.

| | situation cards | first attempt | now |
|---|---|---|---|
| rise | 180px | 24px | 180px |
| tilt | −8deg | none | −8deg |
| scale | 0.82 | none | 0.82 |
| fade | linear, first half | 600ms, full | 550ms linear |
| duration | 1.1s `--ease-in-out` | 600ms `--ease-out` | 1.1s `--ease-in-out` |
| stagger | 0.18s | none | 180ms |

The duration now matches the flip exactly, so the turn and the settle are one
movement. The stagger applies only at `md` and up, where the grid is three
across and the cards genuinely arrive together; stacked, each already fires on
its own trigger and a delay would be lag rather than rhythm.

### It re-arms

The observer no longer disconnects. When a card leaves the viewport it checks
*which way* it went: `boundingClientRect.top > 0` means it is below, so the
reader scrolled up off it and it resets to play again. Leaving upward is
ignored. That asymmetry is the same one `use-situation-cards.ts` makes with
`onEnter` and `onLeaveBack` — returning *down* to something already read should
not rewind it.

### The hover was not smooth, and the cause was the reveal

`[data-appear] > *` set the `transition` SHORTHAND, and globals.css is imported
after Tailwind's utilities, so it overwrote `transition-property` on the same
element the card's hover was using. Rotate and scale had no transition at all
and snapped. The reveal now sits on the wrapper and the hover on the card
inside it, so the two never write the same property on the same element.

### The link

The whole card is clickable through a stretched overlay on the title —
`after:inset-0` — so the accessible name stays the article's title rather than
every word on the card. That is the pattern the situation cards already use, and
their note explains why: two links to the same place mean a screen reader
announcing the destination twice.

**It points at `/insights/`, the index, because there is nowhere else.**
`app/insights/` holds only `page.tsx`, and the static articles carry no slug.
`insightsHref` in navigation.ts says so, so whoever builds the article route has
one place to change.

## The pixel wipe's ellipses, fixed at the root

Reported twice — first at a 30svh band, then again on a wide screen. The first
time it was treated by changing the layer height until the numbers happened to
land inside the cell budget. That fixed the case in front of us and left the
mechanism intact, so it came back as soon as the aspect went wide again.

**The fault is `pixelGrid`'s fallback.** When no arrangement fits the ±20%
cell-count budget — which any wide box hits, because every candidate reaches the
32-column clamp — it derived rows from the COUNT (`target / columns`). That
holds the number of cells and lets the cells themselves go oblong, which is the
opposite of what the function is for.

Rows now come from the ASPECT (`columns / aspect`), so squareness is kept and
the count gives way instead:

| viewport | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| 3440x1440 | 32x9, ratio **1.34** | 32x7, ratio 1.05 |
| 3840x1600 | 32x9, ratio **1.35** | 32x7, ratio 1.05 |
| 5120x1440 | 32x9, ratio **2.00** | 32x5, ratio 1.11 |

`pixelGrid` is shared with the showreel, so that was checked first: from a 337px
phone to a 2560px desktop, at both the 144 and 64 targets, the showreel's search
always finds a candidate and never reaches this branch. Results are identical
either way.

## The CTA band reads white on its own wipe

"Explore insights" stands on cream with dark ink, and brand orange wipes across
it — leaving dark-on-orange, reported as hard to read.

**The component had already written down why the obvious fixes are wrong.** A
`group-hover:` colour flip changes the whole line at once, so for the length of
the sweep the half the wipe has not reached is wearing the wrong ink.
`tone="inverse"` does not apply either: it assumes the band *stands on* orange,
whereas this one stands on cream, where white would vanish at rest.

So the words are drawn TWICE, once per ink, which is exactly what that note
prescribed. The second copy lives inside the wipe panel and carries an equal and
opposite translate, so it is revealed precisely where the fill has arrived —
never a frame early or late, because it is the same element moving. A tone whose
ink suits both surfaces declares no `inkOn` and renders nothing extra, so
`inverse` is untouched.

### Two misses, both invisible to every check available here

1. **The stacking.** The resting label carries `relative` "so it paints above
   the wipe" — correct for one ink, and exactly what hid the second. The fill
   must COVER the resting label so the copy riding on it takes over. Fixed with
   `z-10` on the panel.
2. **The clipping.** The counter-transform holds the second copy still at ALL
   times, including at rest, where the panel is parked at `-100%` and the copy
   is pushed `+100%` — landing it exactly over the band. With nothing clipping
   it the white label sat permanently over the dark one, and the band read white
   on cream before it was ever hovered. Fixed with `overflow-hidden` on the
   panel.

Both times the markup was verified and reported as done, and both times the
markup was right while the *result* was wrong. Rendered HTML cannot show
stacking or clipping. The owner's screenshots were the only thing that caught
either one.

The geometry was then worked through at three points rather than trusted: with
the panel at `-(1-p)W` and the copy at `+(1-p)W`, the copy's absolute position is
always 0 while the panel's clip box is `[-(1-p)W, pW]` — empty at rest, the left
half at p=0.5, the whole band at p=1.

### Resolution — the pixel wipe's cleanup

The Safari Timelines recording asked for above named the owner. Full evidence is
in `docs/audits/2026-09-17-showreel-startup-delay.md`; in short:

- **The stall was a rendering stall inside one synchronous task.** For about six
  seconds the CPU sat near 100% with zero paints, composites or animation
  frames, and nothing but `invalidate-styles` → `recalculate-styles` →
  `forced-layout` in lockstep, ~230 cycles a second.
- **97% of CPU samples** were in GSAP CSSPlugin's `getComputedStyle` wrapper.
  Followed down through React's commit, the caller was `usePixelWipe`'s cleanup:
  `gsap.set(cells, { clearProps: "all" })`.
- For each cell, clearProps clears the inline style and then re-reads the
  computed transform. Write-then-read forces a full-page layout, once per cell:
  ~1,256 across the page's two wipes at ~4.3ms each on the device.
- It ran on load because the effect depends on `columns` and `rows`; the grid
  settles after the first timeline is built, so that timeline is torn down at
  once.

This reconciles the earlier traces. The frame probe's "0ms forced layout"
measured the points it was placed at, not this cleanup; the 5,810ms render wait
it recorded was this task.

**Fix:** the cleanup now removes GSAP's inline properties with plain
`removeProperty` calls and drops GSAP's `_gsap` cache. Writes alone cannot
force a layout, so the browser batches them into one recalculation.

**Confirmed by the owner on the iPhone against `npm start`: the ~5.4s freeze is
gone and the entrance plays immediately.**

## The pixel wipe, redrawn on a canvas

Reported as choppy while scrolling — stutter rather than delay.

**The cause is structural and was not separately measured.** Each wipe was up
to 683 DOM circles on a desktop (618–683 across common sizes; 288 on a phone),
and GSAP wrote a new `transform` to every one on each scroll frame, with no
layer hints. The maths was trivial; the cost was restyling and repainting
hundreds of rounded shapes sixty times a second.

**`will-change: transform` on every cell was considered and rejected.** It would
have created around 1,300 GPU layers across the page's two wipes — a well-known
way to trade stutter for memory pressure and compositing overhead, and iOS
Safari is strict about layer budgets.

**The change:** each wipe is now one `<canvas>`.

- ScrollTrigger only reports progress between the same start and end. With no
  animation attached, `onUpdate` hands that progress to a draw function — the
  mapping `scrub: true` gave the old timeline.
- Every circle goes into ONE path with ONE fill. They share a colour, and
  overlapping ellipses traced the same way fill as their union, so batching
  changes nothing visible.
- `axis: "y"` gives every circle in a row the same delay, so scale is computed
  once per row, not once per circle.
- The colour comes from a hidden element carrying the `surface` class, read once
  per setup. Both wipe tokens are plain hex, so it resolves to `rgb(...)`, which
  every canvas accepts.
- The backing store follows the device pixel ratio, capped at 2 as the
  showreel's canvas is.
- Same fail-safe as before: no JavaScript, a failed import or reduced motion
  leaves the canvas transparent.

### The stagger was reproduced exactly, and checked against GSAP itself

The old wipe used GSAP's grid stagger. The installed `distribute()` in
gsap-core.js resolves `{ each, grid: [rows, cols], axis: "y", from: "end" }` to

    delay(row) = each × rowCount × (rowCount − 1 − row) / (rowCount − 1)

— a total spread of `each × rowCount`, not `each × (rowCount − 1)`. That was
read from the source, then confirmed by calling the real
`gsap.utils.distribute` in Node against the new `rowDelay`: **2,623 cells
across six grid shapes and both lattices, largest difference 4.6 × 10⁻⁸**,
which is GSAP's own rounding. The formula that would have been written from
memory, `row × each`, puts the top row of a 10-row grid at 1.125 against GSAP's
1.25 — a 10% change in the wave's pace, avoided.

### What the rewrite removes

- **The per-cell cleanup that froze the page on load** (earlier today). There
  are no per-cell styles, so there is nothing to clear. The fix recorded above
  is superseded: the code it corrected no longer exists in any form.
- **The rebuild on load**, previously outstanding. The grid was React state, so
  settling it re-rendered every cell and tore the timeline down. A resize now
  re-measures and redraws.
- **About 1,300 DOM nodes** from the page on a desktop, about 580 on a phone.

## Fast scrolling on a phone

Asked whether every animation lagging during a fast fling is normal.

**Partly.** iOS runs momentum scrolling on its own thread, and every animation
here is driven by JavaScript reading the scroll position on the main thread,
which learns the position a frame or more late. So during a fast fling the page
moves first and the animations catch up. That trailing is inherent to
JavaScript-driven scroll animation on iOS. Lenis does not change it, because it
deliberately leaves touch scrolling to the platform.

**What is not inherent is how far behind they fall.** Visible stutter means each
frame costs too much, so the main thread falls further behind mid-fling. That is
the part being addressed.

Options recorded, in the order they should be tried:

1. **Cheaper frames** — the wipe above, and the paper plane below. No downside.
2. **`ScrollTrigger.normalizeScroll()`** — GSAP's documented remedy. It moves
   touch scrolling onto the main thread so animations stay in step, at the cost
   of scrolling no longer feeling native. Try on the device before adopting.
3. **CSS scroll-driven animations** run where the scroll runs and cannot trail,
   but they suit only simple effects, and support on the owner's iOS version was
   not checked.

## The paper plane — a hypothesis, nothing changed

Also reported as laggy. The structure suggests why, but it has not been measured.

The plane is a `<g>` inside ONE inline `<svg>` that fills the whole host section
and extends 15% past each side (`top-0 bottom-0 -inset-x-[15%]`). GSAP's
MotionPathPlugin rewrites that group's transform on every frame, and a browser
cannot composite part of an SVG on its own — so moving a small plane plausibly
repaints a section-sized SVG each frame.

An `opacity` on that SVG was first suggested as a second cost. It is not one:
`PLANE_OPACITY` is 1, which creates no transparency group. Withdrawn.

**Proposed, pending evidence:** move the plane into its own small element the
GPU can move without repainting, and leave the large SVG holding only the
invisible route for MotionPathPlugin to measure. That SVG would then never
change, and never repaint.

**How to confirm first:** a Safari Timelines recording while scrolling through
the section. Paint records carry the painted rectangle (`quad`), so a
section-sized paint on every frame would settle it.
