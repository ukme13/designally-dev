## Brand foundation

Designally is a creative agency in Bangkok with a strategy-led approach to
branding and design. Its purpose is to turn ideas into practical work that
reflects a brand's true identity. It supports businesses through Creation,
Growth, and Transformation.

The core principle is to offer only what really benefits the client.
The four values are:

- Simplicity Is Our Standard.
- Creativity With Purpose.
- Understanding Drives Everything.
- Built to Last.

The 2026 tagline is: **More Than Creative. We Build What Matters.**
Keep its wording when using it as a tagline. It need not appear on every page.
Explain the actual service nearby; the tagline alone does not explain the offer.

The Creator and Magician archetypes guide the team's thinking. In copy, express
them through imagination, practical reasoning, and calm confidence. Avoid
literal magic language or grand promises of transformation.

## Start with the client's reality

Open substantial marketing sections with the client's situation, question, or
desired progress. Then explain how Designally can help. Do not force this
pattern onto navigation labels, factual definitions, or contact details.

| Audience | Situation to recognise | Useful emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transformation Mandate — ideal | The business has changed, but its brand has not caught up | Reasons for change, continuity, and stakeholder decisions |
| Growth Friction — primary | Something is not working; the owner is unsure whether branding is the cause | Diagnosis, priorities, and a clear reason to invest |
| Institutional Buyer — ideal | A marketing team needs a dependable partner and internal support | Clear rationale, responsibilities, and approved process details |
| Exacting Founder — secondary | A founder needs a partner who understands their ambition | Collaboration, design reasoning, and respect for their knowledge |

These are internal planning groups, not labels to publish. Persona names,
quotes, revenue bands, and fears are research or strategy inputs, not real
customer testimonials. Do not publish internal exclusion labels or assume a
reader's age, income, gender, or background.

## Voice rules

| Principle | Apply it in writing |
| --- | --- |
| Direct but warm | Get to the point while respecting the reader's uncertainty. |
| Confident, not loud | Explain choices and show evidence. Avoid superlatives. |
| Empathy first | Name a concrete situation instead of saying “we understand you.” |
| Simple, not simplistic | Use short sentences and explain important reasoning. |
| Honest, not salesy | State what the work can help with and what is still unknown. |
| Ally, not authority | Offer a clear next step; do not lecture or pressure. |

Use active voice and concrete words: clarify, build, plan, test, explain,
design, support. Prefer one main idea per sentence. Explain technical terms
when clients need them. Avoid generic claims such as “world-class solutions,”
“unlock your potential,” “revolutionary,” or “the best agency.” Wit is optional;
clarity and care come first.

## Message pillars

- **Foundation Before Output:** connect design choices to business needs,
  audience understanding, and a clear direction.
- **We're There at Every Moment:** explain support through Creation, Growth,
  and Transformation using services that are actually available.
- **Simple Is How We Work:** explain steps and decisions plainly. Do not turn
  the strategy's “no surprises” language into a contractual guarantee.
- **We Think Like Owners:** show care through practical priorities and tradeoffs.
  Do not promise revenue growth or claim responsibility for all client success.

Choose the pillar that fits the page. Do not repeat all four in every section.

## Tone and language

Website copy should be confident, clear, and strategic. Landing pages should
name the problem quickly and offer an easy next step. Insights can explain
strong opinions with evidence. Social copy may be more casual. Proposals and
client messages should be direct, structured, and caring.

Follow the requested page language. For English, use familiar business language
that Thai readers with functional English can understand. For Thai, use natural,
professional language rather than stiff corporate translation. Preserve the
meaning and tone rather than translating sentence by sentence. Keep approved
names and service terms consistent. Do not add bilingual routes or switch a
page's language unless that is part of the task.

## Examples for direction, not approved copy

| Avoid | Better draft direction |
| --- | --- |
| “We are a world-class agency delivering innovative solutions.” | “Your business has changed. Your brand should help people see what it has become.” |
| “We guarantee a brand that boosts sales.” | “Make it easier for customers to understand what you offer and why it matters.” |
| “We understand every business.” | “Before deciding what to design, we look at what is working and what needs to change.” |
| “Transform your future now!” | “Tell us what is changing in your business.” |

Use CTA wording only when its destination or action exists. Do not imply a
free consultation, audit, fixed delivery time, or response promise without an
approved offer.

## Evidence and publication

The strategy mentions “6 years,” “150+ brands,” a free audit, fixed timelines,
and one contact. These need current evidence or operational confirmation
before publication. Their appearance in the strategy is not sufficient.

Never invent client names, quotes, outcomes, staff, awards, prices, contact
details, or legal promises. Use existing shared facts in `app/_lib/` and approved
planning records. Distinguish delivered work from measured business results.
Record the source, date, and publication permission for material claims.

For missing evidence, use a specific internal note, for example:
`Draft — confirm current brand count and supporting project list.`
When implementing unfinished content, follow the repository's draft-notice
and indexing rules. Do not present sample copy as an approved claim.

## Before handing over copy

Check the audience, client-first opening, relevant message pillar, natural
language, evidence, and real CTA destination. For website content, also use
[SEO, AEO and GEO guidance](SEO-AEO-GEO.md). Report unresolved claims clearly.
